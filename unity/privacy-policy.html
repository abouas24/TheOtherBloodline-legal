Privacy Policy

The Other Bloodline · version 2026-10-17

The short version

The Other Bloodline keeps your game on your device. If you turn on cloud save, we also keep your latest save on our server so it can follow you to a new phone, and an older copy that we check later saves against, including for fairness in the Arena; both are deleted with your account. We hold no password and no email address; the only thing that names you to us is the player ID Google gives us when you sign in with Play Games.

Cloud save is optional. Signing in to Google Play Games is what makes it possible, and you can turn it off, or delete the copies we hold, at any time from Settings.

Some data does leave your device: to Google Play when you make a purchase (and a report of the purchase to Unity Technologies, whose purchasing code the game uses), to Google Play Games while you are signed in, to our own server if you turn on cloud save or join a syndicate held there, to the ad companies when the game can offer an optional rewarded video, and to our own gameplay analytics. Each is described below, and the analytics can be switched off.

What is stored on your device

Your warband, progress, settings and player name are saved on your device. With cloud save off, they never leave it unless you copy the save out yourself, from Settings, under Backup, or your device backs it up to your Google account, as described below. With cloud save on, the same save is sent to our server — see "Cloud save" below.

If your device backs up app data to your Google account, Android's backup includes what the game keeps on the device — your save, your settings and the answers you gave when you first opened the game, including your year of birth while the game still holds it. That backup is held by Google under your Google account and Google's privacy policy; we never receive it, and it comes back only to a device signed in to that account.

Your player name is shown to the members of a syndicate you found or join on our server, to the leaders of a listed Syndicate you ask to join (with the deepest stage you have reached, until your request is answered, withdrawn or expires), and, if you use World chat, to anyone reading it, and, if you have chosen to show it on the Richest Hoards board, to every player who opens the board while your Hoard is listed there, and, if you play on the Arena ladder, to every player who opens the standings of your division or meets your warband there, and, while your warband is among the ten at the top, to every player who opens the Arena standings, and to nobody else; the game tells you so before you found or join one. With cloud save on it is also part of the saves we hold.

Uninstalling the app removes the copy on your device. It does not remove the copies on our server — delete those from Settings, under Account, or from the deletion page linked from this policy.

Advertising

Rewarded videos are optional. A video plays only when you tap a button that says so, in exchange for an in-game reward; the game never shows an ad you did not ask for, and it plays the same if you never watch one. An Adventurer's Pact subscriber is given the same rewards without the videos.

The videos are chosen by Unity LevelPlay (ironSource Mobile Ltd., a Unity company), which runs an auction between two ad networks: Unity Ads (Unity Technologies) and Google AdMob (Google). LevelPlay includes its Ad Quality tool, which records which ad was shown so that ads breaking the networks' content rules can be found and blocked.

To choose, show and measure an ad, these companies may process your device's advertising identifier and Android's app set identifier, an approximate location derived from your IP address, information about your device and this app (such as its model, operating system and language), and how you interacted with the ad. Their software in the game collects this directly; we do not send them your save, your player name or our analytics.

Ads are filtered to remove mature content: Google AdMob is set to a maximum ad content rating of T (Teen) — G on a device where the year given tells us a player is under 13 — and Unity Ads blocks ads rated 17+. The game asks your year of birth once when first opened; it is not directed at children.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland you are asked for consent before the ad software starts. In US states with a consumer privacy law you can opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information and of targeted advertising. Change either choice at any time in Settings, under Privacy, with "Ad privacy choices". You can also reset or delete your device's advertising identifier in Android's settings.

Each company's handling of this data is governed by its own policy: Unity's, which covers LevelPlay, ironSource and Unity Ads, at unity.com/legal/privacy-policy, and Google's at policies.google.com/privacy, with how Google uses information from apps that use its services at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Google Play Games

The game signs you in to Google Play Games when your device allows it. You can decline, and the game plays identically without it.

While signed in, the game can see your Play Games display name and player ID so it can show you which account is connected. The game does not send either to us; if you turn on cloud save, Google tells our server your player ID, as described below.

Google Play Games keeps its own records of how you use it, under Google's privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy, and you control them from your Google account.

Cloud save

Cloud save is optional and starts switched off. The game plays identically without it; the only thing it changes is whether your progress can be recovered on a new device.

To use it you sign in with Google Play Games and turn it on in Settings, under Account. Google tells our server your Play Games player ID, and that ID is how we know which save is yours. We never receive your password, your email address, or your Google account details, and we do not store your Play Games display name.

While cloud save is on, the game sends your save to our server about once a minute while you play, and once more when you close the game. The save contains everything the game knows about your progress: your warband, currencies, stage, purchases, settings and your player name, and, if you have blocked anyone in chat, the in-game name each blocked player was showing when you blocked them.

Our server compares each save you send with the last one it received and the time between them. It also keeps an older copy of your save, replaced at most once a day, and compares a newer save with it once that copy is a day old, and whenever you play on the Arena ladder, to check fairness there. It keeps a note when the difference is larger than the game could have produced. That note is used only to find bugs and to prevent cheating. An unusual save never deletes your save or your account, but it may keep you off the Arena ladder until it is resolved; if that happens, write to us with your support code.

Our server also keeps a short record when two copies of your save disagree and have to be combined, and when a device first sends its save — when it happened, how the copies were combined, and which version of the game sent it — so that we can help if something goes wrong with your save.

Each cloud save account has a support code, shown in Settings. It is a short random code that lets you name your account to us in an email without telling us who you are. It unlocks nothing on its own.

When cloud save is on, our server asks Google Play to confirm each purchase, including ones made before you turned it on, and keeps a record of it — what you bought, Google's order number, when, and whether it was a test purchase — for as long as you have a cloud save. If Google Play later tells us a purchase was refunded, canceled or charged back, we note that on the record, with when it happened. A cosmetic that only that purchase paid for is then no longer shown to other players; for a subscription we ask Google Play whether it is still active, and what it pays for stops showing only once Google says it has ended. For a one-time purchase we never store the purchase token itself, only a one-way fingerprint of it that lets us recognize the same purchase twice. For a subscription we also keep its purchase token, so that our server can ask Google Play whether the subscription is still running. Deleting your cloud save deletes this record too.

Your saves are kept for as long as your cloud save account exists. The sync record and the cheat-check notes are kept for up to 90 days, and whatever is still held goes when you delete your account. Turning cloud save off stops the syncing; the copies already on our server stay until you delete them — see "Deleting your cloud save" below.

Our server runs on Cloudflare, which stores this data on our behalf and is not allowed to use it for anything else. Cloudflare's privacy policy is at cloudflare.com/privacypolicy.

Your cloud save is kept separately from the gameplay analytics described below. The two are never joined: the analytics identifier is not stored with your save, and your player ID is not sent with analytics.

The Arena ladder

If cloud save is on and you play on the Arena ladder, our server keeps your Arena record: a snapshot of the warband you defend with (its creatures, their power and their bonuses), your rating, wins and losses, and your fight tickets, and the league and division your warband stands in this season.

Other players see your defense — that warband, its power and your rating — under your in-game name, with the name and emblem of your Syndicate if you are in one held on our server and the name plate, banner, title and frame you wear and the skins your defenders wear; never your Play Games name. The Arena standings show the same beside your rank, league, division, rating and power (and, to your division, your wins and losses), to every player who opens the standings of your division, and, while your warband is among the ten at the top, to every player who opens the standings; the ten at the top are rebuilt every hour, so they can go on showing your warband for up to about an hour after it leaves them. The weekly standings show your warband under a handle the game makes up from two random words, never your name, with your rank, tier, rating and power. If a device that remembers a child's year of birth from the first-launch question has opened your save, and that save has since reached our server with cloud save on, the warband stands under a handle the game makes up from two random words instead, on every device; what you wear stays beside the handle. A name on the standings must follow the rules in the Terms of Use: any player who sees it can report it from the standings, and we can reset a name that breaks them, as in a syndicate.

Each fight is recorded — when it happened, which defense or computer opponent it was against, and the result — and that record is deleted after 28 days; it is also used to limit how often one warband can be fought. Your Arena record is kept until you delete your cloud save account; the standings of past seasons, with the name shown on them, are kept for three seasons. Your places on past weekly standings are kept until you delete your cloud save account too, which removes them with your Arena record.

The Hoard board

If cloud save is on and your Hoard has opened, our server keeps its own count of the days you have pledged to it: the day it first saw your Hoard open, and the days it has credited since, counted on its own clock. That count can be lower than the one on your Hoard panel — the server credits nothing from before it first saw your Hoard — and it does not go down as you play.

The hundred largest counts are shown to every player who opens the Richest Hoards board, each with a rank, the credited days and the rung they reach. Your place is shown under a handle the game makes up from two random words (a different handle from the one the Arena can show you under) unless you choose to show your in-game name — never your Play Games name. The game asks you before it shows your name, and until you say yes your place stays under the handle. You can turn your name off at any time in Settings, under Account, and turn it on there whenever the board can be reached; after you turn it off, the board can go on showing the name until its next refresh, up to about an hour. A name on the board must follow the rules in the Terms of Use: any player who sees it can report it from the board, and we can reset a name that breaks them, as in a syndicate. If you are not among the hundred, only you see where your count would place you. Your Hoard record, the name it shows and your place on the board are deleted with your cloud save account.

Syndicates

You can found a syndicate, name it, choose its emblem, and invite other players by sharing an invite code. A syndicate held on our server needs Google Play Games sign-in and cloud save; without them you can still found one that lives only on your device.

Membership of a server syndicate lives on our server so that every member sees the same syndicate. A syndicate's name, emblem, level and number of members are public: other players see them in the weekly standings, and our server shows them to any player who asks for that syndicate.

In the member list, the other members of your syndicate see your in-game name (the game gives you one, and you can change it in Settings), your rank, a short code cut from a random member ID, and how long you have been a member. The game tells you this before you found or join one. Your Google Play Games name is never shown. Your in-game name is seen by the members of your syndicate and, if you ask to join a listed Syndicate, by that Syndicate's leaders together with the deepest stage you have reached, until your request is answered, withdrawn or expires; nobody else sees it, except as described under 'Chat', 'The Hoard board' and 'The Arena ladder'. Any player who opens the Syndicate list sees a listed Syndicate's name, emblem, level, number of members, how it takes members and its last Reckoning standing -- never its members' names; a Syndicate's name and emblem are also shown beside each of its members shown by name on the Arena standings. If you are removed from a Syndicate, our server keeps for seven days a record that you may not rejoin it from the list; the record does not hold your name. The request and the removal record are deleted with your account. Members of your Syndicate also see the title and banner you wear; every player who sees your warband on the Arena ladder sees them too, with the name plate and frame you wear and the skins your defenders wear. Members of your Syndicate also see your power (the highest you have sent into the Feud), the calendar day (UTC) you last played, the fronts you took and the strikes you made in this week's Feud and, when they open your card, the name plate and frame your Arena defense carries and the five monsters you set as that defense, in the order you set them, with their rarity, levels, stars and skins. We never send them your gear, your gems, your purchase records or your Google name; a cosmetic sold only for money is shown only while a purchase pays for it, so a member who sees one can tell you bought it. We send them the day, never the time; a member who keeps your Syndicate's page open may see it change to today after you first play that day.

Members can post short messages chosen from a fixed list in the game, and react to each other's posts. Members can also talk in Syndicate chat, described under 'Chat'. Each post shows the in-game name of the member who made it. A syndicate keeps its newest 50 posts, and a post is deleted after 90 days at the latest. The game also posts short activity lines in your Syndicate's chat: a member joining, leaving, being removed, promoted or moved down, a new notice, a strike, and the week's result when your Syndicate placed. A line names the member it is about by their current in-game name only while they are still a member; a member who has left or been removed is shown as 'A member'. The lines are kept for 30 days like other messages.

The leaders of a syndicate can post one short typed notice that its members see. It is checked by the same rules as names, any member can report it, and we can remove it.

Syndicate names, in-game names shown in a syndicate and notices must follow the rules in the Terms of Use. We check them automatically before they are accepted, any player who can see one can report it, and we can reset a name or remove a notice that breaks the rules. A name we reset cannot be used by the same player in a syndicate again, so we keep a record of it until that player deletes their cloud save account. When you report something, we record that the report was yours, so that each player's report counts once. You can also hide another member: their name and their posts are not shown to you on that device, until you show them again. Hiding is kept on your device only, so it is lost if you uninstall the game.

Our server records each assault you make in the weekly feud — which front, the result and the power of the warband you sent — and what your syndicate scored, so that the week can be settled fairly for everyone in it. The feud's rival syndicate is generated by the game; it is not another group of players.

If you leave a syndicate, or are removed from it, your membership and your unused invite codes are removed. The syndicate itself, and its name, stay with the members who remain; if you were its last member it is disbanded, and its name is removed and freed for others. Your assault records, your posts and reactions, a notice you set, any record that we reset your name, and your reports are kept until you delete your cloud save account (posts sooner, as above). Deleting it removes your membership and your name with it, and a syndicate you lead passes to its highest-ranked, longest-serving member.

If you ask to join a Syndicate, its leaders see your in-game name and the deepest stage you have reached. A Syndicate's name, emblem, level, size and last Reckoning standing are visible to any player who opens the Syndicate list, if its leaders chose to list it; its name and emblem are also shown beside each member shown by name on the Arena standings.

A request to join a Syndicate is kept for up to 30 days; the name you sent with it is removed as soon as the request is answered, withdrawn or expires. If you are removed from a Syndicate, a record that you may not rejoin it is kept for seven days. Both are deleted with your account.

Purchases

Purchases are processed by Google Play. We never see or store your payment details.

The game records what you have bought in your save, so it knows what you own; with cloud save on, it is part of the saves our server holds. The purchasing code in the game is Unity's, and it also reports each purchase — and each one that fails, or is still completing when the game starts — to Unity Technologies for its purchase analytics: what was bought, the price, Google Play's order details, a random installation ID and session ID, and your device model, system version and language. Unity holds that under its own privacy policy; this version of the game cannot turn it off.

Analytics

We collect gameplay events — for example that a stage was cleared or a screen was opened — to understand where the game is confusing or unfair.

Each event carries a random install identifier. It is generated on your device the first time you play, it is not linked to your name, your email, your Google account or your advertising identifier, and it lets us tell "one player returned five times" from "five players played once". It does not identify you as a person, and it is destroyed when you switch analytics off or uninstall the app.

Events also carry no name, no email, no contact details and no advertising identifier.

We keep each event for 90 days from the day we receive it; once it is more than 90 days old it is deleted automatically, normally within a day. Three kinds of event are kept for one year instead, because they are how we count whether players come back and whether purchases are made: that a play session started; that the game was opened, together with a few counts of your progress at that moment (the stage you are on and the deepest you have reached, the days you have played, how many times you have rebirthed or transcended, how many creatures you own, and roughly how much gold you hold); and that a purchase succeeded — which pack, its price, how much you have spent in the game altogether and how many purchases that makes, and whether it was a test purchase. Every event carries the random install identifier described above, an identifier for the play session it belonged to, and the number of days since the game was first installed on that device — and nothing that names you.

When you watch a rewarded video, we also record which ad network showed it, which offer it was for, and the amount the ad platform estimates that one video earned us — usually a few cents or less, in US dollars — so that we can check the ad companies' reports against our own. That record carries the same random install identifier as every other event and nothing from the ad itself: no advertising identifier, no location, and no auction or creative identifier.

You can switch analytics off entirely in Settings, under Privacy. When it is off, nothing is sent and the identifier is discarded. The game plays identically either way.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13. If the year you give tells us you are under 13, the ads shown on that device are not personalized. Whatever year you give, what the game collects is as described in the rest of this policy.

When you first open the game we ask your year of birth once; what we do with it is described under 'Chat'.

If the year you give tells us you are under 13, you can use quick chat only.

If you believe a child has provided us with data, contact us and we will delete it.

Chat

Chat is optional. The game is complete without it, and nothing in it is locked behind talking to anyone.

To use chat you sign in with Google Play Games and accept the chat rules. Messages show your in-game name (the game gives you one, and you can change it in Settings), never your Google name, together with your Syndicate's name, emblem and level and how long you have been a member.

Syndicate chat is read by the members of your Syndicate; World chat by anyone in the game. Assume anything you type is readable by everyone in that room.

When you first open the game we ask your year of birth once. It stays on your device until the first time you use chat on the account you first play with; then we send it with your chat terms acceptance and keep it for as long as your account exists, and it decides whether you can type messages or use quick chat. Another account on the same device is asked again. Whether the year you gave was a child's is remembered on the device for as long as the game's data is. Messages are checked before delivery by an automated classifier that runs on Cloudflare, and a second check for harassment and self-harm uses OpenAI's moderation service; neither stores your messages or uses them to train. We keep messages for 30 days and report records for one year. If you are under 18, you confirm that a parent or guardian permits you to use chat.

Quick chat sends a line chosen from a fixed list, not text you typed.

If you report a message, or a message you sent is reported, we keep a record of that report and of any action taken for up to one year. That record is what justifies a mute or a ban, and it is kept even if the message itself has been deleted.

A line that one of the automated checks flags for review, or refuses because it involves a minor or self-harm, is kept with the reason for up to one year so that a person can review it, even if it was never delivered.

A report that our automated checks identify as involving a minor or self-harm is kept until a person has reviewed it, even if that takes longer than a year.

Blocking someone is enforced on our server, not just hidden in your app. A blocked player's messages are not sent to you at all.

Chat data is deleted with your cloud save account, from Settings under Account or from the deletion page linked from this policy; one record is kept for a year, described under 'Deleting your cloud save'.

Deleting your cloud save

In the app: Settings, then Account, then Delete my cloud save, and confirm. This deletes the saves we hold, the sync record, the cheat-check notes, your purchase records, your Hoard record, your Arena record, your syndicate membership and the name it showed, your posts, reactions and any notice you set, your feud records, the invite codes you made and the reports you sent and your support code, together with your chat messages, your chat block list, your chat terms acceptance with the year of birth you gave, any mute or ban on your chat, and the reports you made about other players' messages, immediately and permanently, and switches cloud save off on that device. Your progress on the device is not touched.

If you no longer have the app, email us your support code and we will do it for you. The deletion page linked from this policy explains what we will ask you to confirm first.

If you delete an account that used chat, we keep for one year a record that cannot be traced back to you by anyone but our server: your year of birth as you gave it, whether your chat access had been removed or paused, and the reports other players made about your messages, including the message each one reported. We keep it so that a player whose chat access was removed cannot return by deleting and re-registering, so that the age you gave cannot be changed by re-registering, and to meet legal duties to preserve reports of serious misconduct. It is deleted a year after your account, or sooner if you ask us to.

After a deletion we keep one thing: a record that the player ID was deleted, and when, and — only if the Arena ladder was closed to it at that moment or in the 30 days before — the date it was last closed, or the deletion date if it was closed then. It contains nothing else. It stops an old copy of the game from putting the deleted save back, and stops deleting from being used to get back onto the Arena ladder.

Your choices

Switch analytics off in Settings, under Privacy.

Change your consent to personalized ads, or opt out of the sale or sharing of your data, in Settings, under Privacy, with "Ad privacy choices"; reset or delete your advertising identifier in Android's settings.

Turn cloud save on or off, and delete the copies on our server, in Settings, under Account.

Delete everything on this device by uninstalling the app.

Ask us anything, or request deletion of anything you believe we hold, at ToBsupport@gmail.com.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the app will tell you the next time you open it. The version date shown with this policy tells you which one you are reading.
