Deleting your data

This page explains how to delete the data associated with The Other Bloodline, published by Alexander Bouas.

You do not need an account, a login, or the app installed to use anything on this page.

If you would rather just ask, email ToBsupport@gmail.com and we will handle it.

There is no account to delete

The Other Bloodline does not issue accounts. We never created one for you, we hold no password, and we have no profile with your name, email or contact details in it.

Signing in is optional and uses Google Play Games — your own Google account, not one we made. We cannot delete a Google account, and you would not want us to be able to.

What follows is every place data connected to this game can exist, and how to remove it from each.

Step 1 — cancel any subscription first

If you have an active subscription, cancel it before deleting anything. Deleting your save does NOT cancel a subscription, and you would keep being billed for something you can no longer use.

Cancel it in the Google Play Store app: tap your profile picture, then Payments and subscriptions, then Subscriptions.

If you are unsure whether you have one, that screen is also where you find out.

Step 2 — delete your game data

Everything the game knows about you lives on your device. Two ways to remove it, both immediate and both permanent:

In the app: Settings, then Reset Save. This erases your warband, progress, settings, player name and analytics identifier.

Or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes the same local data.

We hold no copy of your save, so once it is gone we cannot restore it. There is no undo and no support route that recovers it.

Step 3 — delete the cloud save, if you made one

If you linked Google Play Games, a backup of your progress is stored by Google under your own Google account using their Saved Games feature. We never receive it and cannot read or delete it.

Delete it from the Google Play Games app: open it, go to the game, and delete the saved game. You can also unlink the game from your Google account there.

Google describes their own data controls at policies.google.com/privacy.

Step 4 — ask us to delete the analytics data

If you left analytics on, we hold gameplay events — that a stage was cleared, that a screen was opened. They carry no name, no email, no contact details and no advertising identifier.

They are keyed by a random install identifier generated on your device. To have them deleted, open the app, go to Settings and then Privacy, copy the analytics ID shown there, and email it to ToBsupport@gmail.com asking for deletion.

We need that ID because it is the only thing the records are keyed by. Without it we genuinely cannot find your rows, and that is also the reason those rows do not identify you as a person.

If you have already uninstalled and no longer have the ID, there is nothing left that can be traced back to you. You can still email us and we will confirm that.

We will action a request within 30 days.

What we cannot delete, and why

Purchase and refund records. If you bought something, Google Play and our billing provider RevenueCat hold a record of the transaction. Those are kept for tax, accounting, fraud-prevention and regulatory reasons, and neither we nor you can erase them. They are not deleted by any step above.

Data held by Google. Advertising data processed by Google AdMob, and any cloud save held under your Google account, are governed by Google and controlled from your Google account, not by us.

Aggregate counts. Numbers that no longer refer to any individual — how many players cleared a stage in a given week — remain, because there is nothing in them to delete.
